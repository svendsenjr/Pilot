# Nordic Property Maintenance Platform - Investment Thesis (Screening Memo)

**Sector:** Property maintenance / janitorial services (vaktmester, eiendomsdrift, fastighetsskötsel)
**Geography:** Norway-led, Nordic platform optionality
**Reference point:** PHM Group (Norvestor VIII, since 2017)
**Prepared by:** [Deal team], KLAR Partners
**Date:** May 2026
**Status:** Screening memo - decision: proceed to Phase 2 origination on conditional basis

---

## 1. Recommendation

**Conditional proceed.** Pursue Phase 2 origination targeting two to three named platforms (Vaktmesterkompaniet, Insider Group, Din Vaktmester) over the next two quarters. The macro thesis is robust; the platform-availability thesis is fragile and is the gating risk. We should NOT pre-commit a fund slot until management access on at least one Tier-1 candidate is secured.

**Headline view in three lines:**
- The Norwegian property maintenance market is structurally attractive (NOK 15-25bn TAM, recurring, fragmented, regulation-shielded), and PHM at <5% share leaves >95% of the market open for a credible challenger.
- The investable universe behind PHM is materially thinner than the market structure implies. Three of the top six potential platforms are already sponsor-owned (Tyro/Norvestor, Cares/Equip, Polygon/AEA), the two largest are listed (ISS, Coor), and the remaining family-owned candidates have known transition obstacles.
- Right to win rests on KLAR's existing Nordic services playbook (Nimlas, Ocab) and a thesis that residential janitor services (vs. commercial IFM) is a defensible white-space PHM cannot fully cover at its current trajectory. This is the credible angle - not "out-roll PHM at scale."

**What would kill this deal:**
- Inability to land a Tier-1 platform anchor at <8.5x EV/EBITDA within 18 months.
- PHM accelerating to >10% Norway share via a single transformative deal (e.g., Tomagruppen carve-out).
- Norwegian residential service-tax regulation or labour reform that reprices the route economics.

## 2. Market Thesis (Why this market, why now)

### 2.1 Market size and structure

| Sub-segment | Norway TAM (NOKbn) | Source / basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily janitor / building care (vaktmester, eiendomsdrift) | 8 - 12 | Sector codes 81.10 / 81.21, residential + light commercial; analyst estimate triangulating ~12k registered AS entities |
| Cleaning (industrial + residential) | 12 - 16 | Statistics Norway services sub-sector, BoldData top-50 aggregate (~NOK 20bn FY23) |
| Outdoor / grounds / snow (utomhus) | 2 - 4 | Estimate; meaningful seasonal but lumpy revenue |
| Light technical FM (HVAC, electrical service, plumbing visits) | 5 - 8 | Adjacent; Nimlas-relevant, partial overlap |
| **Addressable for this platform (excl. catering, security, heavy IFM)** | **15 - 25** | Brief baseline; consistent with bottom-up |

Sweden (fastighetsskötsel) adds an estimated SEK 25-35bn equivalent in the same definition; Denmark and Finland together add roughly DKK/EUR equivalent of NOK 15-20bn. Pan-Nordic TAM for the addressable segment is NOK 55-80bn.

Growth: nominal 4-6% p.a. (population growth + housing stock expansion + wage-driven price-throughput). Real growth 1-2%. Below GDP but with high visibility.

### 2.2 Structural attractiveness

| KLAR sector test | Read | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission-critical | Pass | Snow clearing in winter and building safety inspections are legally required; cleaning frequency mandated by health regulation |
| Non-discretionary | Pass | Demand persists through cycle; 2020 COVID stress test showed cleaning volumes resilient, janitor stable |
| Recurring revenue | Pass | 70-85% under multi-year framework agreements (typical 2-3 years, auto-renewing); housing co-op (borettslag) contracts especially sticky |
| Pricing power | Partial | Indexed to KPI or wage index in most contracts; net pricing power constrained by labour cost pass-through. Real margin expansion via density, not price |
| Fragmentation | Pass (defining feature) | Top 5 players <25% market share; ~12,000 registered cleaning/FM entities in Norway; PHM at <5% |
| Capex-light | Pass | Working capital and small fleet; <2% revenue maintenance capex |
| Cash conversion | Pass | 75-90% EBITDA-to-FCF after WC |
| Cyclicality | Partial | Insulated overall but exposed to construction cycle for new contract origination |
| Regulatory shield | Pass | Allmenngjøringsloven (general application of collective wage agreements) creates labour-cost floor that disadvantages sub-scale informal operators - i.e., consolidation tailwind |

### 2.3 Why now

- **Generational handover wave.** Two-thirds of family-owned mid-sized Norwegian FM businesses (NOK 100-500m revenue) were founded 1985-2000 with founders now aged 60+. Insider Group's 2024 Mitie carve-out and Sefbo's sale to PHM are both signals that owner appetite is opening up.
- **PHM playbook proves the thesis.** Norvestor has demonstrated in real time that this geography and segment can absorb a Nordic roll-up at scale; the question is now structural (#2 slot) not directional (does the playbook work).
- **Bolt-on supply still abundant.** Below PHM and below the named platforms, the long tail of NOK 30-150m revenue local players (>200 names in our screen) is largely untouched.
- **Multiple compression in adjacent labour-cost services has stopped.** 2022-2024 saw entry multiples compress from 11-13x to 7-9x on cleaning assets. The market is at a reasonable entry point.

### 2.4 What would make us walk away from the market

| Pushback | Severity | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Norwegian housing co-op (borettslag) tendering culture creates downward price pressure on contracts | Medium | True for cleaning; less true for janitor where switching costs are higher (key holding, knowledge of building) |
| Labour cost inflation eats sector margin | Medium | Allmenngjøring forces all competitors to match - i.e., it disadvantages cowboys more than us. Margin pressure is symmetric |
| AI / smart-building dis-intermediation | Low | Janitor work is physical; remote monitoring adds work, does not remove it |
| Carbon / electrification driving customer mix shift | Low | Indifferent to fuel mix; modest opportunity from EV-charging maintenance |
| Regulatory wage hike beyond index | Medium | Pass-through is contractual but lagged; can compress margin 100-200 bps in a step year |

**Net read:** Market thesis passes the KLAR sector test. This is the same downside-protected, fragmented, recurring-revenue territory KLAR has historically targeted.

## 3. The PHM Effect - reading the incumbent

### 3.1 PHM's trajectory

- Founded as a Finnish platform in 2017 under Norvestor VIII.
- Group revenue estimated EUR 700-850m FY24 (PHM does not publicly disclose; triangulated from public filings of Norwegian subsidiaries plus Norvestor commentary).
- 30+ Norwegian acquisitions since 2017, including Sefbo (2022, the prior largest Norwegian janitor roll-up).
- Norway sub-platform estimated NOK 1.8-2.4bn revenue, i.e. roughly 5-10% of the addressable Norwegian segment per our TAM definition - somewhat higher than the <5% in the task brief if we exclude pure cleaning from the denominator, but the order of magnitude holds.
- Hold period: Norvestor VIII vintage 2017. Exit window is open NOW. Process likely 2026-2027.

### 3.2 What PHM does well

- Residential housing-association focus (borettslag, sameie) - the niche the brief identifies. ~70% residential.
- Local sub-brand retention post-acquisition - preserves relationships, no "PHM-ification" of front-line.
- Disciplined bolt-on pricing (4-6x EBITDA for sub-NOK 50m targets; 6-8x for sub-NOK 200m).

### 3.3 What PHM does NOT do (the white space)

- **Light commercial and public-sector.** PHM's residential focus leaves municipal, healthcare, retail-property, and office-park janitor contracts under-penetrated. This is roughly 30-40% of the TAM and growing.
- **Technical service integration.** PHM does not meaningfully bundle HVAC service, electrical, or plumbing visits - which housing managers increasingly want as a single contract. This is the Nimlas adjacency.
- **Sweden at scale.** PHM has Swedish operations but they are sub-scale; the Swedish fastighetsskötsel market is largely untouched by a Nordic-led roll-up.
- **Damage / restoration adjacency.** Property managers are routinely the buyer for water-damage call-outs. PHM does not have a restoration arm. This is the Ocab adjacency.

### 3.4 Implication for a #2 platform

Direct head-to-head against PHM in residential janitor in Eastern Norway = price war we lose. The credible thesis is **adjacent positioning**:

> Mid-market commercial property janitor + light technical + restoration handoffs, with selective residential expansion, anchored on a Nordic geographic footprint PHM has not built.

This is not "PHM but cheaper." It is a structurally different customer mix that converges on the same TAM by a different route.

### 4.3 Top three platforms - detailed read

#### Vaktmesterkompaniet AS (Oslo)
- **Business:** Norwegian-owned janitor and building-care platform; ~1,000 employees; Oslo / Akershus / Østfold density.
- **Founders:** Original founders still operationally active; minority external capital absent.
- **Financials (estimated):** Revenue NOK ~1bn FY24, EBITDA margin 7-9% (range NOK 70-90m), revenue CAGR 22-24 ~12% (mix organic + small bolt-ons).
- **Why it works:** Service mix mirrors PHM's residential-focused playbook. Founders aged ~55-65, generational handover live. Bolt-on track record (10+ small acquisitions) shows integration capacity.
- **Why it might not:** Founders may favour strategic acquirer over sponsor; entry multiple likely 9-11x given category competition; PHM has presumably approached - we are at best #2 in the queue.
- **Indicative entry:** EV NOK 700-1,000m at 9-11x; cheque size ~NOK 500-700m equity for ~70% post-leverage.
- **Validation gates before IC:** (a) financials in Proff.no / unaudited management accounts; (b) founder posture on sale (advisor/management approach in Q3 2026); (c) confirm PHM is not in advanced dialogue.

#### Insider Group AS (Høvik)
- **Business:** Multi-service FM platform - cleaning, janitor, light technical, canteen. Recent Mitie Norway carve-out (2024) added scale and signals M&A appetite.
- **Ownership:** Family-controlled; secondary investors possible.
- **Financials (estimated):** Revenue NOK 1.0-1.1bn FY24 (pro-forma Mitie); EBITDA margin 6-8% (NOK 70-90m); CAGR 22-24 elevated due to Mitie deal.
- **Why it works:** M&A muscle demonstrated. Mitie carve-out integration is a real-time stress test of acquirer capability. Family stewardship paired with execution discipline.
- **Why it might not:** More IFM than property maintenance per se; cleaning content >50% of mix, which is a different segment than PHM. Risk that the platform's centre of gravity drifts away from the property-maintenance thesis.
- **Indicative entry:** EV NOK 700-950m at 9-11x; cheque ~NOK 500-650m equity.
- **Validation gates:** (a) Mitie carve-out P&L stabilisation; (b) family appetite for further dilution vs. retention; (c) is the platform leaning more cleaning-IFM or more janitor (the latter is the thesis fit).

#### Din Vaktmester AS (Trondheim)
- **Business:** Mid-Norway PHM analog; pure janitor / property maintenance mix.
- **Financials (estimated):** Revenue NOK 250-350m FY24; EBITDA margin 8-10% (NOK 20-35m).
- **Why it works:** Genuine PHM-style positioning. Mid-Norway is structurally under-served by PHM (which is Oslo-anchored). Could be the right anchor for a Norway-wide rollup if combined with Insider or Vaktmesterkompaniet.
- **Why it might not:** Sub-scale on standalone basis. EV cheque of NOK 200-300m alone is too small to drive a fund; only works as part of a string-of-pearls or as bolt-on to a larger anchor.
- **Indicative entry:** EV NOK 200-300m at 8-10x; would only proceed as part of a larger platform construction.
- **Validation gates:** (a) management quality and integration capability; (b) whether founder is sale-ready; (c) competitive positioning vs. local PHM subsidiaries (e.g., Sefbo's Trondheim presence).

### 4.4 The honest pushback on platform availability

The thinness of the platform set is the real risk. Three observations:

- **Of six candidates, only two (Vaktmesterkompaniet, Insider) are credible standalone platforms at the scale KLAR needs (>NOK 800m revenue, >NOK 60m EBITDA).** The others are bolt-ons or long-shots.
- **Both Tier-1 candidates will have multiple bidders.** Norvestor (via PHM), strategic IFM (ISS, Coor, Compass), and other Nordic mid-market sponsors (Adelis, FSN, Verdane, Equip) all in scope.
- **A failed approach on both leaves us with a "build the platform from three sub-scale assets" path that is more expensive, slower, and has a worse risk-adjusted return than the headline thesis suggests.** This is the realistic downside case and must be priced in.

## 5. KLAR framework application

### 5.1 4KQ - Four Key Questions

#### Q1. Why this market?
- TAM NOK 15-25bn (Norway); >NOK 55-80bn (Nordic). Fragmented (top-5 <25%). Recurring (70-85% framework). Regulation-protected from cowboys (allmenngjøring). Recession-resilient (proven in 2020).
- Growth 4-6% nominal; below GDP but high visibility and pricing pass-through.
- Verdict: **Pass with conviction.** This is exactly the kind of sector KLAR has historically targeted (mission-critical, fragmented, recurring, services).

#### Q2. Why this asset?
- Conditional on landing one of two named Tier-1 platforms.
- Asset quality strong in absolute terms (recurring, sticky, capex-light) but the *available* asset universe is thin.
- Verdict: **Conditional pass.** We need Phase 2 origination to convert the thesis from market-level to asset-level.

#### Q3. Why now?
- Generational handover wave is real (founders 60+).
- PHM exit process likely 2026-2027 - creates a market opportunity (more banker attention) but also a competitive risk (Norvestor will defend optionality).
- Multiples have rebased from 11-13x to 7-9x on labour-cost services.
- Verdict: **Pass.** Timing is genuinely favourable. Window is 18-24 months.

#### Q4. Why KLAR?
- **Nordic services playbook:** Nimlas (technical), Ocab (restoration) - both directly adjacent and validate KLAR's right-to-win in labour-cost services consolidation.
- **Cross-pollination:** Nimlas provides technical service bundling capability; Ocab provides damage-restoration handoff. Neither sits in a property maintenance platform today.
- **Operational toolkit:** KLAR's playbook on route density, procurement consolidation, digital scheduling, and bolt-on integration applies directly.
- **Sweden access:** Ocab gives us a Swedish front door that PHM does not have.
- Verdict: **Pass.** Right-to-win is articulable and defensible. The portfolio synergy is real, not synthetic.

**4KQ aggregate:** 3.5 / 4. The asset question is the gating one - converts on management access.

### 5.2 Triple A

#### Attractive market
- Score: **A.** Fragmentation, recurrence, regulation, downside protection all present. Growth moderate but visible.

#### Attractive business
- Score: **A- (conditional).** Asset quality strong but contingent on landing a Tier-1 platform. Sub-scale anchor + bolt-on construction would degrade this to B+.

#### Attractive entry / exit
- Score: **A-.** Entry multiples currently 7-9x for category, with founder transitions creating off-market opportunity. Exit clear: next sponsor (Adelis, Triton, IK), strategic (PHM if they're still around, or international like Atalian / Mitie), or IPO at scale (>NOK 5bn EV).

## 7. Risks and pushbacks (institutional honesty)

### 7.1 Risks we can manage

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labour cost inflation outpacing index | Medium | Quarterly price reviews, density-driven productivity |
| Bolt-on integration failure | Medium | Sub-brand retention, route-by-route integration playbook, dedicated PMI capability |

### 7.2 Risks that gate IC

| Risk | Severity | Why this could kill the deal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inability to land Tier-1 platform anchor at <9.5x | High | Without a real anchor, this becomes a sub-scale rollup that does not justify a fund slot. We must walk if we cannot get Vaktmesterkompaniet, Insider, or a comparable named platform |

| Norvestor mid-life recap of PHM instead of exit | Medium | If PHM is recapped not exited, our exit comp narrative weakens. Mitigated by other sponsor exits in adjacent labour services |
| Allmenngjøring regulatory tightening / minimum-wage shock | Medium | Symmetric across competitors but creates a one-off margin step-down |

### 7.3 Pushbacks we expect at IC

| IC objection | Counter |
| --- | --- |
| "This is just a cheaper labour services play - no real moat" | True at unit level, false at platform level. Moat is route density + procurement scale + bolt-on integration capability. Same logic that drove PHM's value creation under Norvestor |
| "Why isn't PHM already doing this? They have first-mover advantage" | PHM has chosen a residential focus and Eastern Norway density. Commercial property and pan-Nordic positioning is a different platform construction; PHM cannot do both at once without margin disruption |
| "Adjacencies to Nimlas / Ocab feel synthetic" | Cross-referral, not equity integration. Realistic synergy 30-50 bps EBITDA margin. We should not over-claim |
| "Exit market is sponsor-to-sponsor - that's a thin universe" | True, but the universe is 6-10 credible bidders (Triton, IK, Adelis, Cinven, etc.) plus strategics. We have run this exit profile before |
| "Multiples look optimistic at exit" | Entry 8-9x and exit 11-13x reflects scale rerating plus margin expansion. If we exit at flat 9x, IRR drops to 12-15% and MoM to ~1.8x - still investable but tight |

### 7.4 The honest deal-killer

If, after Phase 2 origination over Q3-Q4 2026, we have not secured management access at Vaktmesterkompaniet OR Insider, we should park this thesis. A platform built from Din Vaktmester + 5 small bolt-ons is not enough margin of safety relative to PHM's lead.

## 11. Concluding view

The macro thesis is correct: there is a structurally attractive #2 platform opportunity behind PHM in Nordic property maintenance, supported by market fragmentation, regulatory tailwinds, recurring revenue, and active generational handover. KLAR has a credible right-to-win via Nimlas and Ocab portfolio adjacencies, sector-services playbook, and Nordic geographic coverage.

The honest weakness is platform availability. The investable universe is two named candidates plus three long-shots, not the long roster the market structure implies. We should advance to Phase 2 origination immediately on Vaktmesterkompaniet and Insider Group, and **walk decisively if neither converts to management access within two quarters**. A consolation strategy built from sub-scale anchors is not the right deployment for this fund's mid-market platform slot.

**Decision sought from screening committee:** Approval to dedicate one full-time origination resource and a modest sector commercial diligence budget (NOK 1.5-2.5m external) over Q3-Q4 2026, with mandatory re-screening checkpoint at end-Q4 2026 conditional on Tier-1 management access.

## Appendix C - Glossary (Norwegian terms)
